What this is

You’re not logging workouts.
You’re climbing a mountain.

Switchback is a coaching app for people training toward a physical objective — a big hike, a peak, an expedition, or just getting genuinely strong and lean. It hands you a structured plan — as long as your goal needs — tells you exactly what to lift and how much, feeds you day by day, points you at real trails on the weekend, and wraps the whole thing in a single running metaphor: every session you finish is vertical feet climbed toward Everest’s summit.

Most fitness apps are a blank spreadsheet with good fonts. Switchback is the opposite — it has an opinion. It knows what Tuesday of week 11 should look like, what weight you should probably put on the bar, how many calories to eat to reach your goal weight on schedule, and which trail near you matches this weekend’s prescribed climb. Your job is to show up and do the work; the app carries the planning.

Why the mountain metaphor

Consistency is the entire game in a months-long program, and abstract numbers don’t hold people. Turning “I completed a workout” into “I climbed 217 ft, my streak is a blaze, and I just passed Denali” makes the boring middle weeks feel like progress you can see. The altitude, ranks, camps, and badges all exist to get you to the final week.

The three things it does

Programs the training

A periodized plan — strength, cardio, and a weekend ruck — that automatically suggests working weights, adds load as you get stronger, and backs off during deload weeks.

Runs the nutrition

A calorie & macro target computed from your body and paced to hit your goal weight by the final week, plus a fast food log with barcode scanning.

Finds the terrain

A weekend ruck prescription matched to a real trail near you, with the trailhead forecast, an elevation profile, and one-tap logging.

Keeps the score

Altitude, streaks, ranks, world-peak milestones and 17 badges — a whole progression layer so a long training block stays motivating.

Good to know before you start

The whole app is offline-first. Everything records on your phone instantly and syncs to the cloud when you have signal — so it works on a mountain with no bars. Signing in is optional; you can tap Train offline and start immediately. This guide walks the app roughly in the order you’ll meet it.

01Base camp · Getting in

Sign in — or don’t

An account exists for exactly one reason: to sync your log across devices and keep it backed up. It is never a wall between you and training. The first screen you see offers four ways forward.

  1. Email me a sign-in linkType your email and tap the button. We send a “magic link” — tap it on this phone and you’re in. A brand-new email creates your account automatically; there’s no separate sign-up.
  2. Enter the 6-digit codeThe same email also contains a 6-digit code. Use it when the link opens on the wrong device, or when you just prefer typing. This is the reliable fallback.
  3. Use a password insteadPrefer a classic email + password? Tap Use a password instead and sign in that way.
  4. Train offline — sync laterSkip all of it. The app runs fully local; sign in any time later and your history uploads on first sync.
Why magic link first

No password to invent, remember, or leak — the safest default for most people. The code is there for the real-world case where the email opens on your laptop but you’re holding your phone at the gym.

Switching accounts on one phone

The phone holds one person’s data at a time. If a different account signs in, the app wipes the previous user’s local data first and re-downloads the new one cleanly — so a shared phone never mixes two people’s logs.

Why an 8-rep weight, not a max

The calibration field asks for a weight you could do for about 8 clean reps with 2–3 left in the tank — never a true one-rep max. Testing a max cold is how people get hurt. From that submaximal number the app estimates your working max and prescribes everything conservatively from there.

You can re-open this screen any time from the slider icon in the header (top-right). Re-calibrating never resets your calendar or your streak — it just refreshes the suggested weights and fuel targets going forward.

The plan itself

The program at a glance

Under the metaphor is a real, periodized strength-and-conditioning block — and its length is yours. Name a trip and the plan runs from today until that date (an 8-week floor, up to a full season); leave it blank and it defaults to a 26-week build. Either way the shape is the same: three phases, six training days, and a weekend ruck, with a deload every 4–5 weeks so you recover and adapt.

Three phases

Each phase has its own color throughout the app — you’ll see it on the ridgeline in the header, on phase pills, and on the ascent diagram.

BASE CAMP · foundation & volume LOAD CAMP · heavier, longer ALPINE PUSH · peak & sharpen

On the default 26-week plan the phases run weeks 1–9, 10–18, and 19–26. When you set an objective the phases rescale proportionally to fit your length.

A week of training

The weekly split is constant — you always know that Wednesday is a big lower-body day and Saturday is the ruck. Cardio days sit between the strength days on purpose, to spread stress and build the aerobic base a long day in the mountains actually needs.

Deloads & the taper

Deload weeks (about every 4–5 weeks) cut every lift to 2 sets at lighter loads — the app does this automatically and tells you on the day. If you set a trip, the final week becomes a taper: same easy treatment, so you arrive fresh instead of fried. Skipping a workout on a deload week won’t break your streak (that’s the “shield”).

04Session Mode · The workout

One pitch at a time

Session Mode is the heart of the app — a distraction-free, full-screen space for the workout itself. Each exercise is a “pitch” (a climbing move up the route). You focus on one at a time and swipe the rail to move between them. The screen stays awake the whole time so it never sleeps mid-set.

What each pitch gives you

  • The prescription — target sets × reps and a suggested working weight in gold.
  • Warm-up ramp & plate math — for barbell lifts, a ladder of warm-up sets and exactly which plates go on each side. No mental arithmetic under the bar.
  • Field-guide notes — muscles worked, cues, “why we do it”, step-by-step form, common faults, and safety flags for the big lifts.
  • Set logging — a row per set with weight and reps you can edit, and a big green check to mark each one done.
  • Last time & progression — what you did last session, plus a sparkline of the exercise trending up over recent sessions.

The rest timer

Check off a set and a rest countdown starts automatically, sized to that exercise. A band shows the time draining; you can +30 s or Skip. It also schedules a lock-screen notification so you can put the phone down between sets and still get pinged when it’s time for the next one.

Non-loggable pitches

Cardio, carries, mobility and “protocol” items don’t have a weight to track — they show a single Mark done button instead of set rows.

Summit — banking the day

Past the last pitch you reach the summit summary: duration, sets completed, total volume, feet earned, and any new personal bests (★) you set. Tap Bank +N ft · Complete to mark the day done, feed your altitude and streak, and — if connected — mirror the session into Apple Health. New badges you cross trigger a celebration on the way out.

Why sets are frozen at the start

Your suggested weights are computed from history before today and locked when the session opens, so logging a set mid-workout can’t reshuffle the numbers you’re looking at. What you see when you start is what you lift.

Under the numbers

How the suggested weight is chosen

Every gold weight in the app comes from the same small, transparent engine. It starts conservative and then learns from what you actually lift.

  1. Seed a working maxIf you calibrated a lift, the app converts your ~8-rep weight into an estimated max (with an extra cushion for “New” lifters). If you didn’t, it estimates from your bodyweight and experience level.
  2. Prescribe from the maxEach set’s target is a percentage of that max, adjusted for the rep count — heavier for 5s, lighter for 12s.
  3. Self-correct from historyOnce you’ve logged a lift, real data overrides the estimate: hit all your sets → the next suggestion goes up by a small increment; a bad miss (under half the reps) → it drops ~10%; a partial miss → it holds.
  4. Back off on deloadsDuring deload and taper weeks every suggestion is automatically reduced.
Last sessionNext suggestionWhy
Hit every set at target reps+ incrementYou earned more load.

Why suggestions, not commands

These are conservative estimates, not prescriptions. The app says so on the calibration screen: start light, add weight only when every rep is clean, and stop any set where form breaks down. You can always override the number in the set row — the app just learns from whatever you actually log.

06Fuel tab · Eat to the plan

Fuel

Training builds the engine; food decides whether you lose the weight. Fuel gives you a daily calorie target and macro split, a fast log, and a searchable food database with barcode scanning.

Your targets

If you entered fuel stats in onboarding, the app computes your maintenance calories (Mifflin–St Jeor BMR × an activity factor that already includes your six weekly sessions) and a daily target with a deficit paced to reach your goal weight by the final week — from your latest logged weight. Fall behind and it widens the deficit; get ahead and it relaxes. It’s clamped to a safe band and never dips below your BMR.

Protein · 1 g per lb of goal weight Carbs · fill the rest, around training Fat · ~30% of calories

No fuel stats? You get sensible default targets and a nudge to add them. Nothing breaks.

Logging what you eat

  • Daily total — type the day’s calories and tap Log (200–12,000). A 7-day list shows each day vs. target and a running average.
  • Food lookup — search your personal library first (instant, offline), then USDA’s database. Each result shows calories per serving.
  • Barcode scan — tap SCAN, point at a package, and Open Food Facts fills in the nutrition.
  • Editable before logging — every picked food shows an editable calorie value (crowd-sourced data is a draft, not gospel). Tap + Add to add it to today.
Your library learns

Every food you log is saved to your personal library and its use-count climbs, so your regulars float to the top and you rarely hit the network. It all works offline; searches just quietly return nothing until you’re back on signal.

Below the log sits a food guide (protein anchors, carbs around training, measured fats) and a sample day — reference material for building meals that hit the targets.

The killer feature

The Objective — one trip reshapes everything

Leave the trip blank and you run the default 26-week program. Name a real trip — a name, a date, and a few numbers — and Switchback re-plans the entire block around it, from today until your trip date.

Calendar
The program now ends on your trip date, and the final week becomes a taper so you arrive fresh. Phases and deloads rescale to fit. (held to an 8-week minimum)
Saturday rucks
Pack weight and elevation ramp toward your objective — building to your stated pack (plus a margin) and biggest-day gain by the peak weeks.
Cardio
Zone-2 and threshold minutes re-scale to the phase; a max altitude of 12,000 ft+ adds extra aerobic volume to prep for thin air.
Exercises
Your trip style swaps in specific movements — Technical adds grip, calves and dead hangs; Expedition favors carries and heavy step-ups.
Why this matters

A generic program prepares you for nothing in particular. Telling Switchback “I’m climbing Kilimanjaro on August 14 with a 30 lb pack” turns a stock plan into a countdown built for that day — the rucks get heavier toward the summit, the aerobic base grows for altitude, and the plan lands you at the trailhead peaked, not exhausted. You set it once in onboarding (or later from the header slider), and every tab quietly adjusts.

Note: a trip date must be in the future — the app flags a past date rather than silently collapsing your calendar.

The plumbing

Under the hood

You never have to think about any of this — but here’s what’s happening so you can trust it.

Offline-first & sync

Your phone is the source of truth. Every action writes to local storage instantly, so the app is fully usable with zero signal — exactly the condition you’re in on a mountain. When you have connectivity and you’re signed in, changes sync to the cloud in the background and pull down anything you did on another device. Conflicts resolve by most-recent-edit, so two devices converge cleanly.

Health is off until you tap Connect Apple Health on the Week tab, and permission is only ever requested from that tap. If you deny read access it looks identical to “no data” — nothing is faked, no line appears.

Notifications

  • Saturday ruck alert — 06:30 Saturday with the prescription, suggested trail, and forecast.
  • Rest timer — a lock-screen ping when a between-set rest ends, so you can pocket the phone.

Both are local notifications — nothing is sent to a server, and they’re silent if you don’t grant permission.

Your data & privacy

Your log is yours. In the cloud it’s protected by row-level security — every record is stamped with your user id, and the database only ever returns your own rows. External lookups (food, weather, trails) go through the app’s own proxy so third parties never see who you are, only an anonymous query.

Field notes

Quick answers

Do I have to sign in?

No. Tap Train offline and use everything locally; sign in later to back up and sync — your history uploads on first sync.

I skipped calibration. Am I stuck?

Not at all. You get conservative default weights. Re-open Calibration from the header slider any time; it never resets your calendar or streak.

Can I change the weights it suggests?

Yes — edit any set’s weight in Session Mode. The app learns from what you actually log and adjusts next time.

I missed a few days. Did I lose everything?

Your altitude and badges are permanent. A missed day breaks your streak — unless it fell on a deload week, which is shielded.

What if I don’t have a barbell?

Choose Dumbbells or Minimal in Calibration. The app only suggests weights for lifts your equipment supports.

Does the food search work off-grid?

Your personal library does, instantly. USDA search and barcode scan need signal and quietly return nothing when offline.

How do I mark a day done without logging sets?

Use the round check on the Today card, or Log this hike on a trail for Saturdays.

Can I change my goal trip later?

Yes — reopen Calibration and edit The Objective. The whole plan re-anchors; your start date and streak stay put.

The one-sentence version

Show up, tap Start Session, do the work, weigh in a few times a week, and take the Saturday ruck outside — the app handles the rest and turns months of consistency into a climb you can watch happen.
